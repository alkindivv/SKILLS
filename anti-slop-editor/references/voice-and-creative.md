# Voice, creative writing, and spoken scripts

## Preserve an author, not a persona template

Use samples the user provides or authorizes. Observe diction, directness,
formality, rhythm, humor, point of view, preferred terminology, and deliberate
quirks. Preserve choices that serve the work. Do not treat a brief sample as a
statistical fingerprint or infer sensitive personal attributes from it.

Match voice without copying distinctive passages. Never invent a first-person
experience, feeling, opinion, credential, or endorsement on behalf of a real
speaker. A requested ghostwritten opinion needs the speaker's position or an
explicitly proposed position for approval. It is not license to fabricate history.

Use `assets/voice-profile.example.yaml` as an optional user-edited reference.
Do not write a persistent profile or overwrite an existing one without consent.
Keep project-specific brand voice separate from a user's personal voice.

## Creative work

Respect genre, viewpoint, pacing, imagery, and purposeful repetition. Fiction
may invent detail because invention is its declared contract; nonfiction may
not borrow that license. In poetry, repeated structure may be the form. In
speech, a three-part rhythm may aid delivery. Keep it when it is intentional.

Improve specificity through coherent setting, character choices, meaningful
images, and consequences rather than random adjectives or ornamental metaphors.
Do not replace every unexpected image with utilitarian prose. Cut a metaphor
when it is incoherent or generic, not merely because it is figurative.

Do not apply "answer first" to a mystery's reveal or a story's opening by force.
Do not add flaws, fragments, slang, or misspellings just to increase variability.
Leave an effective passage alone. Revision is allowed to preserve the original.

## Spoken scripts

Write for listening when that is the medium. Use clear referents, manageable
clauses, and transitions a listener can follow. Avoid reading tables or citation
syntax aloud unless the format demands it. Keep attribution in a speakable form;
retain a separate source list when requested. Distinguish a narrator's proposed
line from something they have personally witnessed.

## Conflict handling

Follow explicit user requirements and source fidelity before this skill's
stylistic preferences. Keep a requested tone without inventing facts. If the
brief asks for "more confident," strengthen organization and supported wording,
not the certainty of evidence. If it asks for "more human," interpret that as
natural, appropriate writing, not deceptive authorship or fake personal detail.
