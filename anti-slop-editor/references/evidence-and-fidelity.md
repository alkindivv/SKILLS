# Evidence and fidelity

## Keep a lightweight claim ledger

For consequential writing, track material claims internally:
`claim | source/location | supported scope | uncertainty | proposed change`.
Track quantities with their unit, denominator, period, baseline, and population.
Do not show a ledger unless requested or needed to resolve an issue.

Distinguish supplied facts, independently checked facts, attributed allegations,
inferences, estimates, proposals, goals, and illustrative fiction. A rewrite of
a supplied statement is not an independent fact-check.

When verification is part of the task, use authorized primary sources and cite
the specific claim. Respect host requirements for current or high-stakes facts.
When research is not needed, do not browse just to decorate a pure rewrite.
Never expose private drafts to a third-party editing service without permission.

## Protect meaning at the smallest consequential span

Preserve "at least," "at most," "up to," "approximately," "unless," "only if,"
"not," "may," "must," deadlines, exclusions, and conditions. Resolve ambiguous
pronouns only when the source identifies their referent.

Do not interchange percent and percentage points, revenue and profit, average
and median, forecast and actual, sample and population, or correlation and cause.
Do not convert units, currencies, dates, or timezones without authorization and
a reliable conversion basis. Keep a documented precision/rounding policy.

Keep citation placement attached to the supported assertion after restructuring.
Retain pinpoint pages, footnotes, hyperlinks, and quoted wording when relevant.
Do not insert an impressive-looking citation that was never consulted.

## Handle bad or incomplete source material

- Translation: preserve source meaning; separate any correction or uncertainty
  note from the translated text. Do not silently "improve" the source's facts.
- Proofreading/line editing: preserve substantive claims, and flag important
  unsupported ones. Do not silently erase inconvenient content.
- Authorized substantive editing: explain material corrections or removals;
  preserve the evidence and reasoning behind the original position.
- New writing: use only supplied or checked specifics. Omit a nonessential
  unsupported assertion, narrow it, or ask for evidence needed to retain it.
- Summary: select by the requested scope, not by whether a point is flattering.
  Keep material caveats and disagreement; mark coverage gaps.

A placeholder such as `[source required]` belongs in an explicitly incomplete
draft, not in supposedly publication-ready copy. Do not label an artifact final
while silently leaving material claims unresolved.

## High-stakes wording

Do not substitute plain-language preferences for technical or professional
meaning. Preserve terms of art and required warnings. Explain an opaque term
beside it where allowed, rather than deleting it. Legal, medical, financial, or
safety-critical review may require a qualified professional; do not certify such
review from this skill alone. Protect personal information when examples are
shared outside their authorized context.
