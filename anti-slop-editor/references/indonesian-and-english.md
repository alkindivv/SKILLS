# Indonesian and English language notes

## Choose register from the artifact, not the chat

Use explicit user direction, audience, publication conventions, and supplied
samples. Do not automatically insert "gue/lo," "nggak," contractions, or jokes
because the user used them while briefing the assistant. Formal, neutral,
conversational, and literary writing can all be natural.

Keep pronouns and address appropriate and consistent unless a deliberate shift
is supported by the author's voice. Preserve intentional code-switching where
it helps this audience. Do not add English phrases to make Indonesian sound
premium, or replace familiar technical terms with awkward literal coinages.

## Indonesian

Use direct verbs when the meaning stays the same: "melakukan pemeriksaan"
can become "memeriksa." Keep a nominal construction when the document needs
it as an established term. Reduce nested clauses that hide the main statement.

Inspect phrases such as "di era digital yang semakin berkembang," "dalam rangka
untuk," "memiliki peran yang sangat penting," and "memberikan dampak positif."
They are candidates for a meaning test, not prohibited strings. Replace them
with the actual activity, effect, or context when supplied. Without that detail,
write a narrower claim or flag the information gap.

Do not automatically replace "selain itu" with "jadi": addition is not cause.
Do not replace "memanfaatkan" with "menggunakan" when the distinction matters.
Keep "signifikan" when it has a defined statistical or substantive meaning.
Do not ban "merupakan," "namun," "di sisi lain," or "berkelanjutan" when used
accurately. A blanket synonym table is not an editing method.

Use "yang mana" and "di mana" according to their actual grammatical role. An
English-style relative-clause calque may need restructuring; a real location
or question does not. Preserve passive constructions when the action's object
is the natural focus, such as "Draf tersimpan otomatis," if that behavior is
established. Do not invent an actor to enforce active voice.

For formal spelling questions, consult the official EYD resources when needed:
https://ejaan.kemendikdasmen.go.id/
Official institutional reference:
https://badanbahasa.kemendikdasmen.go.id/produk-detail/3685/ejaan-yang-disempurnakan-eyd
Do not override the user's deliberate literary or conversational style with
unrequested formalization. EYD is a spelling reference, not a ban on author voice.

## English

Prefer concrete subjects and useful verbs. Replace nominalization only when it
improves comprehension. Use ordinary verbs such as "is" and "has" when accurate;
keep "represents," "serves," or "features" when they express a different meaning.

Inspect inflated phrases such as "a groundbreaking leap," "unlock potential,"
and "a testament to our commitment." Keep specialist words with an actual
specialist meaning. "Robust" in a statistical discussion is not automatically
marketing fog. Do not replace a recurring technical term with creative synonyms.

Use contractions when the context calls for them, not to simulate a human.
Respect regional spelling and the requested house style. Punctuation, lists,
and passive voice are editorial tools; no mechanical quota applies.

## Original register contrast

Same supplied fact: a review is not finished; delivery is proposed for Friday.
Formal: "Peninjauan belum selesai. Kami mengusulkan pengiriman pada Jumat."
Conversational: "Review-nya belum selesai. Kami usulkan kirim hari Jumat."
Neither becomes "Pasti selesai Jumat," which invents a commitment.

## Other languages

Apply the meaning and format protections, then follow target-language grammar
and genre conventions. Do not export English preferences about subject omission,
sentence length, contractions, or punctuation to every language. This bundle
contains detailed ID/EN examples, not validated native style guides for every
language. Treat other-language support as a framework requiring local review.
